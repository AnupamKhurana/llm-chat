Here are some fun and fascinating facts about Indian history across different eras:

---

### **Ancient India**
1. **Indus Valley Civilization (3300–1300 BCE):**
   - The **Indus Valley Civilization** (also called Harappan Civilization) was one of the world’s earliest urban civilizations, alongside Mesopotamia and Egypt. Its cities like **Mohenjo-Daro** and **Harappa** featured advanced drainage systems, granaries, and even standardized brick sizes.
   - Surprisingly, no **tombs** or **monuments** have been found, suggesting that the elite may have been buried at home or in unmarked graves.

2. **Rigveda (Oldest Written Text):**
   - The **Rigveda**, composed around **1500–1200 BCE**, is the oldest known **literary work in the world**. It contains hymns dedicated to gods like **Indra, Varuna, and Agni**, and its language (Sanskrit) is considered the foundation of many modern Indo-European languages.

3. **Wheel Invention:**
   - The **wheel** was invented in India around **3500 BCE**, possibly in the **Indus Valley**. This was one of the earliest known inventions, and its use spread globally through trade and conquest.

---

### **Classical Period and Major Empires**
4. **Maurya Empire (320–185 BCE) – Chandragupta Maurya:**
   - **Chandragupta Maurya** founded the empire after conquering the Nanda Dynasty with the help of **Chanakya** (author of the *Arthashastra*, a book on politics and strategy).
   - The empire reached its peak under **Ashoka the Great** (r. 268–232 BCE), who converted to **Buddhism** after witnessing the bloody **Kalinga War (261 BCE)** and spread the **Dharma (moral code)** globally through his pillars.

5. **Gupta Empire (320–550 CE) – The "Golden Age":**
   - Often called the **"Golden Age of India,"** the Gupta Empire saw advances in **science, mathematics (inventing the concept of zero!), astronomy, medicine (Sushruta was a pioneer in surgery), and literature**.
   - The **Chess game** originated in India during this time, called *Chaturanga* (meaning "four divisions" of armies: elephants, horses, cannons, and infantry).

6. **Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646 CE):**
   - This **Hindu kingdom** in southern India was one of the most powerful in history, rivaling the Mughals. It was famous for its **architectural marvels**, like the **Virupaksha Temple** and **Hampi’s ruins**, which inspired UNESCO’s **World Heritage Site** status.
   - The empire also thrived as a **trade hub**, connecting Asia, Europe, and Africa through its ports.

7. **Maratha Empire (1674–1818 CE) – Shivaji:**
   - **Shivaji Maharatna** (1630–1680) founded the Maratha Empire, one of India’s greatest **independent states**. He was known for his **military tactics, guerrilla warfare, and resistance against Mughal rule**.
   - His **forts** (like **Rajmachi**) were designed with **water defenses** to counter Mughal armies.

---

### **Weird and Unusual Historical Facts**
8. **The "Blind Emperor" of India:**
   - **Prithviraj Chauhan** (1156–1192 CE), a legendary Hindu king, was famously **blind** for his last 20 years. Despite this, he led a powerful **Delhi Sultanate** before losing to **Muhammad Ghori** at the **Battle of Tarain (1192 CE)**.

9. **India’s First Airport (Pre-1930s):**
   - Before modern air travel, **Calcutta (Kolkata)** had an **early airport** in **1910**—the **Air Terminal at Dum Dum**—used for **mail flights** and small planes. The first commercial flight in India took off from **Bombay (Mumbai)** in **1913**.

10. **The Longest War in History (Chola Empire vs. Arab Naval Powers):**
   - The **Chola Empire** fought the **Arab naval powers** for **over 150 years (985–1100 CE)** to protect their trade routes. The **Battle of Tondaimandalam (1014 CE)** was a turning point, where the Cholas used **fire ships** to defeat the Arabs.

11. **India’s First "No-Fly Zone" (Mughal Era):**
   - The **Mughal Emperor Akbar** (1556–1605) issued a **decree forbidding travel to the Himalayas** due to cold weather and snowstorms. This was one of the earliest **environmental regulations** in history!

12. **The "Terrorist" Who Became a Freedom Fighter:**
   - **Veerappan**, a notorious **squatter and smuggler**, was hunted by the Indian government for decades. Eventually, he was **killed in 2004**, but his legend persists as a symbol of **army resistance** in rural India.

---

### **Modern India**
13. **First Nuclear Test (1974) – Pokhran I:**
   - India conducted its **first nuclear test, Operation Smiling Buddha**, in **May 1974** (not 1998 as often believed). It was a **peaceful nuclear explosion** to prove India’s capability, leading to the **Pugwash Declaration** (a global non-proliferation agreement).

14. **Longest-Serving Prime Minister:**
   - **Jawaharlal Nehru** (1889–1964) served for **17 years (1947–1964)**, while **Indira Gandhi** (1917–1984) held the record for **longest continuous tenure (13 years, 1966–1977, 1980–1984)**.

15. **First Woman President:**
   - **Pratibha Patil** became India’s **first woman President** in **2007**, succeeding **K.R. Narayanan**.

---

### **Bonus: Cultural & Everyday Historical Facts**
16. **India’s Oldest University (Nalanda):** Founded in **500 BCE**, the **University of Nalanda** was a center of learning until it was destroyed by **Bakhtiyar Khilji** in **1193 CE**. It was so famous that **Buddhist monks from Tibet and China** traveled thousands of miles to study there.

17. **The "Indian Time" Phenomenon:**
   - Before GPS, Indian Railways used the **"Indian Standard Time (IST)"**, which is **5 hours and 30 minutes ahead of Greenwich Mean Time (GMT)**. This was adjusted in **1981** for better global coordination.

18. **India’s First Movie Star (Prabhat Kumar Mukherjee):**
   - The **first Indian film star** was **Prabhat Kumar Mukherjee**, who appeared in the silent film **"Griha Pravesh"** in **1913**. He also starred in **"Dakghar"** (1914), one of the first talkie films (*Indu*, 1931).

19. **The "Indian Coffee Table Book" of History:**
   - The **Encyclopædia Britannica** famously said India was a **"cradle of civilization"**, but it also claimed it was "now a junior partner in British Empire." This changed in **1947** when India gained independence.
